    The one thing you buy

    Need a costume?

    There is no official store yet. When one is picked, the link lands right here — until then, here is exactly what to look for so you can shop wherever you like. Order early: this stuff is seasonal, and the sizes at both ends go first.

    TBD — costume store link not picked yet

    The Lads

    Dark brown Lederhosen

    Lederhosen — leather knee-breeches. Say it “LAY-der-hoh-zen.”

    Search for a full set — shorts, shirt, socks, little hat — rather than buying the pieces apart. Fourteen of you, one silhouette, no committee meeting. Dark brown is the rule; which dark brown is your business.

    The Ladies

    Blue Dirndl

    Dirndl — the dress-blouse-apron outfit. Say it “DEERN-dl.”

    Search for a three-piece set — dress, blouse and apron together. Blue is the rule; which blue is your business. Every size is often its own listing, so widen the search if yours isn’t showing.

    The Ladies · if you’d rather not wear a dress

    Shorts and a blue blouse

    The grid says dirndl in blue, and the grid wins on the colour — but nobody is going to police the silhouette. Buy the shorts in brown or black — blue leather shorts have never existed — and let a blue plaid blouse carry the blue.

    Sizes run strange in both directions, so check the measurements on whatever listing you buy from. No prices are printed here on purpose: nothing is picked yet, and a made-up number would be worse than none. Any dark brown lederhosen and any blue dirndl works. The grid cares about the colour, not the receipt.

07

15 words, one weekend

German 101

Nobody here speaks German. That is fine. Every word you’ll hear this weekend is below, with what it means and how to say it out loud. Say it with confidence and the accent takes care of itself.

Wurst

Sausage. It is also the joke in “For Better or for Wurst” — it sounds exactly like “worst,” which is the entire gag.

Say it: VOORST

Prost

Cheers. The one you’ll say most.

Say it: PROHST

O’zapft is

“It’s tapped.” What you shout the second the keg is open.

Say it: oh‑TSAHPFT iss

Maß

A one‑litre mug of beer. Heavier than it looks. Two hands is not cheating.

Say it: MAHSS

Tracht

Traditional festival dress — the whole outfit, both sides of the party.

Say it: TRAHKT

Lederhosen

Leather knee‑breeches. The lads, Saturday afternoon, in dark brown.

Say it: LAY‑der‑hoh‑zen

Dirndl

The dress‑blouse‑apron outfit. The ladies, Saturday afternoon, in blue.

Say it: DEERN‑dl

The apron‑knot code — where you tie the bow says something

  • LeftSingle.
  • RightTaken.
  • Front‑centreVirgin, or undecided.
  • BackWidowed — or working: you’re the one serving.

Megan gets the last word on anyone’s knot placement.

Schuhplattler

The Bavarian shoe‑slapping dance. Thighs, knees, soles, in that order, loudly.

Say it: SHOO‑platt‑ler

Prüfung

A test or a trial. Every Beer Olympics event is one, and every one of them is winnable stone-cold sober.

Say it: PROO‑foong

Gemütlichkeit

Warm, cosy belonging. The entire point of the weekend, in one unpronounceable word.

Say it: guh‑MOOT‑likh‑kite

Ein Prosit

“A toast.” The bit where everyone stops talking and lifts whatever they’re holding.

Say it: ine PROH‑zit

Zicke zacke

Means nothing. Genuinely nothing. You still have to shout it.

Say it: TSIK‑uh TSAK‑uh

Bierdeckel

Beer mat, or coaster. The cardboard square under your drink, and the thing everyone builds a tower out of by ten o’clock.

Say it: BEER‑deck‑el

Zapfhahn

The tap itself. There are two of them and one pours root beer.

Say it: TSAPF‑hahn

Currywurst

Bratwurst, sliced, drowned in curry ketchup. The caterer’s whole thing — German Taste Currywurst is feeding us Saturday.

Say it: KUH‑ree‑voorst

That’s the whole vocabulary — fifteen words, every one of them glossed and spelled out above. Nobody is going to test you. Learn Prost and you’ll get through the weekend on charm.
