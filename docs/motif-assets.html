Component A

.mark — the Sanction Mark

The page’s heartbeat, and the only thing that repeats often. An 18px hairline square holding one amber fusil — a diamond taller than it is wide, the correct Bavarian form. It is deliberately the same 45° geometry as the existing .lozenge texture and .tbd::before, so the mark and the ambient paper are one shape at two scales. Goes: first child of every .bulletin (7), top-right of every .card--official (3), flanking the footer seal legend (2). Twelve total, hard cap.

Component B

Goes: inside each of the seven .section-head blocks, as the first child. It replaces both .section-num and .section-kicker — delete those two elements. THE SANCTIONED CURVE. The heart’s two arcs and its round-capped icing dots are the page’s ONE permitted curve, in the same way the old rubber stamp was its one permitted tilt. RULE OF TINCTURE, ENFORCED: metals (cream, amber) never touch metals; colours (blue) never touch colours. The cream chief carries blue fusils; the blue field carries the amber mallet. This 700-year-old rule is also a contrast guarantee, which is why the seal survives the 1-bit test by construction. Gingerbread red is deliberately NOT promoted to a heraldic field — that is the one place this motif could go somewhere ugly. “1810” is the year Oktoberfest was founded, as a wedding party: the committee’s jurisdiction over a bach party is genuinely correct, not a gag.

Guard

The 1-bit test

Toggle this to strip every colour, fill and shadow from the motif. Every device — seal, pictograms, mark, medal table, heart — must still read. If anything needs its colour or its amber to look grand, it was never grand. All of the above pass by construction: single stroke weight, flat fills, no bevels, no tonal steps.

Applies html.bit, which forces every motif fill and stroke to black on white.
